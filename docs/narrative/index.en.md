# Narrative

Story in games is assembled from parts that are older than games: a goal that pulls people
forward, a shape for the journey, a world with its own rules, and a way of saying the whole
idea in one sentence. This section takes those parts one at a time.

It is not a writing course and it is not literary theory. Each page names a device, shows it
working in a game you can actually play, shows it failing, and stops.

## How to read a page

Read the first two sections. If the example already makes sense, skip to *check yourself*
and try the questions. A question you cannot answer tells you which section to reread.

## A warning before you start

None of these pages will make a story good. They are vocabulary and checks: things that let
you look at your own design and ask a sharper question than "is it interesting?". A design
document that uses the words correctly and has nothing underneath is worse than one that
says nothing at all.
